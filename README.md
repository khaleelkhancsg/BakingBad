# Baking Bad

An idle bakery game on the real clock. You inherit Aunt Rosa's bakery stall at the night market, and £24,000 of her debt.
The bank wants £2,000 a week, starting after two weeks' grace. Dez, one of Rosa's regulars, knows what the back room used to be for.

The game never ends: like Clash of Clans, you just keep building the place up. Levels go up to 99.

It's a 3D game: you look down into the shop at an angle. The bakery has ovens, mixers, fryers and more, and a glass case
that fills with what you make while customers walk in and queue. Next door is the back room, lit purple. Tap a machine
(or the bubble above it) to work it. Drag to look around, and pinch or scroll to zoom.

A compact strip along the top shows the time, cash, level, popularity, heat and anything that needs you. Zoom right out to see the whole building from outside (walls, windows, the sign and awning, the roofs); zoom back in for the cut-away view. Tap the door between the shop and the back room (or its 🚪 bubble) to go through. Three buttons sit in the bottom-right corner: **☰ Menu** (in the shop: Kitchen, Supplies, Upgrades, Crew, Orders, Marketing, Shops & property,
Bank & tax; in the back room only back-room sections: Lab, Supplies (kits, cutting, the auction), Upgrades, Crew,
Deals (the back door, Dez's jobs, deliveries), Heat & police, Turf, Wash and Logistics), **🎨 Design** (in the back room: its benches, cellar and annex), and **🗺️ Map** to move between your shops and the back room.

## The real clock

There's no in-game day. The clock in the corner is your clock.

- **The bakery never closes.** Buy ingredients, cook, change prices, hire, decorate and expand whenever you like.
  Customers come all day and night, busiest at breakfast and lunch, quietest in the small hours.
- **The back room** can be used any time, but buyers only come to the back door in the evening, at night and in the
  early morning (6pm to 7am).
- **A new day starts at 6am**: rent and wages are paid, the bank takes its payment on payment day, the police decide
  whether to raid, the market sets new ingredient prices, and yesterday's report is filed (📋 in the HUD, or the Bank tab).
- **When you're away**, the game plays the time forward when you come back: customers buy from the case until it's
  empty, food left done in a machine spoils, interest builds on missed payments, crew do their jobs, and buyers come and
  go at the back door. "While you were away" sums it up. Up to 14 days are played; anything longer is skipped.

## Buying things

- **Ingredients**: use − and + to fill the basket (Stock up adds three batches of everything on sale), then Checkout.
- **Design**: tiles, wallpaper, decor and wall art show in the shop as you place them but are only paid for when you
  press Checkout; Cancel puts everything back.
- **Big purchases** (machines, tiers, expansions, shops, vans, fronts, corners, property, upgrades and recipes) ask
  you to confirm first.

## The kitchen

Every machine has its own menu. Tap one to cook, set prices, choose what's on sale and learn recipes. Each batch is one
timer on one machine. When it's done, tap ✓ to put it in the case. Leave it too long and it spoils: food keeps for about
as long as it took to cook (at least 30 minutes).

Recipes run from a minute or two to twelve hours. Short ones pay best per hour while you're playing; long ones make
bigger batches and cover the hours you're out or asleep. Later recipes sell for more and make more servings.

| 🍳 Fryer, ☕ Drinks machine, 🍦 Ice cream machine | 2, 4, 7 |
| 🥪 Sandwich grill, 🍕 Pizza oven, 🫓 Crêpe griddle | 11, 16, 21 |
| 🎍 Dim sum steamer, 🍜 Noodle bar, 🍫 Chocolate station | 27, 33, 39 |
| 🍖 Smoker, 🧁 Patisserie bench, 🍹 Drinks bar | 46, 53, 61 |
| 🍣 Sushi counter, 🍗 Rotisserie, 🍭 Sweet machine | 68, 76, 84 |
| 👑 Grand patisserie | 92 |

The stall only takes the first four; the rest are fitted in Rosa's branches (see Shops). You can run at most three of any machine in a shop.

205 recipes in all, from scones and lattes to pho, Beef Wellington and a golden croquembouche. You can only own as many of each machine as your level allows, and only as many as you have room for:
expansions (Design → Expand) add more spots, so a room never fills up before it has grown. The bakery starts with 6
spots along its walls; the kitchen refit (level 7) adds 4 between them, the island 2 and the bigger island 2 more (back to
back, with an aisle in front of each row), then food carts and side stalls outside. The back room starts with 4; knocking
into the storeroom (level 14) makes it 3 m deeper with a new back wall (+1), and the benches, more benches, cellar and
annex go in that space and along the side walls. At the limit the buy
button says "Reach level N to unlock additional ovens". The same goes for how many things you can sell at once, how
many kits Dez sells you a day, how many decorations you can place, how many campaigns you can run and how much the bank lends.

When lots of machines crowd the screen, idle ones show as small dots. Crew make idle play work: Priya the baker puts finished food in the case, Marco the head chef starts the same recipe
again whenever its tray has room, and Grace the manager keeps the ingredients topped up.

## The back room

Say yes to Dez and the stall's back room is yours. Every branch you open has a back room of its own with its own
machines, but only the stall has buyers: branch back rooms pack what they make into Rosa's vans, which drive it to
the stall under the bakery's own deliveries (see Logistics). Bags, kits, heat, turf and laundering are shared. Buy kits from Dez, cook them on the back-room machines, and sell the bags to
buyers at the back door after dark. The products are parodies of real ones; the "chemistry" is all game mechanics.

- **Purity** decides what a bag is worth (60% sells at the list price). It comes from the machine's tier, back-room
  upgrades (scales, extractor hood, filters, purity tester), Doc and Pip.
- **Cutting** (Pure, Light, Heavy) gives more bags from a cook, each worth less.
- **Street rep** decides how many buyers come. Pure product raises it; cut or weak product and busts lower it.
  Buyers mostly come for what you're known to have in stock; if you're out, Jay or Lena offers them something else
  and about half take it.
- **Heat** cools by itself a little faster every level (0.27 an hour at level 1, about 1.25 at level 50), so a bigger
  operation can run without the police closing in, as long as you don't push it.
- **Turf** (🗺️): buy corners around town, from the estate to penthouse parties. Give each a product and stock it; they
  sell while you're away, faster at night. Police sweeps and rival crews can take what's on a corner.
- **Dez** buys anything you can't shift, at half price.
- **Night crew**: Jay sells to buyers you miss (keeps 20%), Lena the host does it better (keeps 10%, never serves anyone
  flagged), Ash spots officers, Sam bags finished cooks, Doc starts the next one, Kai keeps the corners stocked, DS Hale
  tips you off about raids, Big Ron sees off rivals.


## Logistics

- **Vans** (Menu → 🚚 Logistics): the first comes with your first branch. Buy more as you level up, make them bigger,
  and fit hidden compartments. A run takes 1 hour from the café up to 4 from the maison.
- **Stop and search**: any run can be stopped (more likely when heat is high or the load is big). If police find the
  load it's seized, the van impounded for two days and the driver arrested. Marnie the dispatcher sends vans for you.
- **Delivery rounds** (level 45): buyers phone instead of knocking and riders take the product to them. Most undercover
  officers never get past the call, there's less heat around the stall, raids are less likely and buyers pay 10% more,
  but riders can be stopped and searched or walk into a sting: the bags are lost and the rider arrested.

## Clean and dirty money

- **Clean cash** pays for everything above board: ingredients, machines, recipes, decor, crew, campaigns, shops and the bank.
- **Dirty cash** comes easier (the back room pays well) but buys less: the back room's machines, tiers and upgrades, the
  night crew, bribes and Dez's kits. Dez charges half as much again if you pay him in clean money.
- **Holding dirty cash is a risk**: raids, rival crews and busts all take a share of it.
- **Laundering is slow and costs a cut.** Each day only about a third of the day's takings across your shops (plus what
  your branches can front) passes as normal; more than that adds heat. The wash takes 25%; Viv the accountant, creative
  accounting and a shell company take less.

## Washing money

Menu → 🧼 Wash lists every route. Each has its own daily limit, cut, speed, tax treatment and risk, so spreading
money across them keeps heat and HMRC's questions down:

| Route | From | Cut | Taxed? | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🧾 Through the tills | 1 | 25% (less with Viv and upgrades) | yes | capped by your real takings; HMRC notice it most |
| 🏧 Smurfing | 8 | 10% | no | slow (trickles in all day, up to a daily limit); banks sometimes flag deposits |
| 🎰 The casino | 25 | 15% | no (gambling winnings aren't taxed) | casinos report big spenders |
| 🏝️ Offshore account | 35 | 5% in, 15% out clean | yes, as consultancy fees | dirty cash parked there is safe from raids, busts and rivals |
| 🪙 Crypto | 45 | 5–40%, changes by the hour | no | an exchange may freeze the account and keep half |
| 🖼️ The art dealer | 60 | 20% | yes | takes three days |
| 🧺 Fronts | 12+ | 8–15% | yes | bought with clean cash; each washes a set amount every night |

Fronts run from a launderette (£1,500 a night) to a nightclub (£200,000). A pile of unwashed dirty cash (over £5,000) counts as extra heat when police decide whether to raid, so washing or stashing it lowers the odds of a bust. HMRC notice front money half as much as
till money; an investigation or a bust can close a front for a few days. Felix the money man spreads your dirty cash
across the casino, cheap crypto and the art dealer every morning. A neat paper trail makes investigations less likely;
a friendly bank manager doubles smurfing limits.

## Tax

Inspired by the UK system, but settled weekly (a real tax year would take a real year). HMRC's bill comes at 6am once
a week and is paid from clean cash; see it building up in the 🏦 Bank tab.

- **VAT (20%)** once your declared takings pass £90,000. Hot food and drinks are standard-rated; cold takeaway food and
  bread are zero-rated, as in the UK.
- **Corporation tax**: 19% on small profits, 25% above the main limit, with marginal relief in between. Ingredients,
  rent, wages, machines and everything else you spend on the business count against profit.
- **Employer National Insurance (15%)** on day-crew wages above the threshold.
- **Business rates** on every branch; the stall gets small business rates relief.
- **Laundered money is declared income**, so it's taxed too.
- **Declaring less** (90%, 75% or half) cuts the bill but raises the chance of an investigation. If HMRC find undeclared
  income you pay it back twice over and it makes the papers. Viv the accountant trims the bill and halves the chance.
- **Unpaid tax** gets a 5% penalty and daily interest, and HMRC take what they can each morning.

## Designing the back rooms

In a back room, 🎨 Design has its own tabs (paid in dirty cash, through the same basket and checkout):

- **Floor** and **Walls**: whole-room styles, from bare concrete and breeze block to lab epoxy, soundproof foam and gold leaf.
- **Decor** on the back room's free squares (machine spots, the doors and where buyers stand stay clear). Pieces do
  something, added up across all your back rooms:
  - 😌 **Comfort** (sofas, a big telly, a pool table, a fish tank, a jukebox): the night crew gain loyalty faster.
  - 🫥 **Cover** (bread crates, flour shelving, a wall safe, a false wall): raids find product less often (up to 40% less).
  - 🛡️ **Security** (CCTV monitors, a reinforced door, a panic room): heat cools faster (up to 50%).
- **Layout**: knock into the storeroom first (the room gets 3 m deeper), then the benches, cellar and annex for more
  machine spots. Decor can't go where it would cut anyone off from a machine, a door or the buyers (those squares show
  faint red); decor already in the way when the room changes comes down with a full refund.
- **Walking**: people walk around machines, counters and decor. If there's no way through they stop as close as they
  can get rather than walking through things.

## Prestige

From level 25, Menu → Bank & tax → Prestige lets you hand the business on and start again from nothing: no shops,
machines, cash or crew, and a fresh debt at Aunt Rosa's stall. In return you get **prestige tokens** for what the
business was worth (the square root of net worth over £10,000: £1M is worth 10, £100M is worth 100). Every token ever
earned adds 1% to every sale and bag, for good. Spend tokens on perks that make each fresh start quicker:

| Perk | Each level |
|---|---|
| 💰 Rosa's savings | start with £2,500 more |
| 🏦 Good credit | inherit £2,500 less debt |
| 🎓 Quick learner | +10% XP |
| 🫶 Loyal regulars | +5% customers |
| 📜 Family recipes | start knowing every recipe up to 5 levels higher |
| 🏷️ Trade discount | machines 5% cheaper |
| 🚀 A head start | start two levels higher |
| 🤝 Old contacts | back room open from day one, 2 more kits a day, kits 5% cheaper |
| 🕶️ Street legend | +10 street rep, +5% on every bag |

Perks cost more tokens with each level. Perks and tokens carry over every reset; everything else (milestones
included) starts again.

## The long game

- **Crew loyalty**: every hire has loyalty and greed (greedy ones cost more). Loyalty grows while they're paid; a bonus
  (a day's pay) buys more. Night crew below 30 may talk to the detective.
- **Decisions** pop up and wait for you (❗ in the top bar): bail for an arrested dealer or driver (pay to keep the crew
  loyal, or cut them loose for less heat), and now and then a bulk deal, a journalist, a health inspector, a package
  Dez wants you to hide, or a charity appeal. Ignore one and the safe option is taken.
- **DI Harrow's case** (🕵️ in the top bar): stings, seized vans and deliveries, corner sweeps, raids, informants and HMRC
  finds all add evidence; lying low lets it fade. At 100% it goes to court: big fines, everything seized, back rooms
  sealed for a week and most of the night crew sent down. Bribe the detective or frame a rival crew to knock it back.
- **Heat by area**: vans and the streets (corners and riders) have their own heat, which cools faster than the back room's.
- **Supplier contracts** (Supplies): ingredients 15–20% cheaper for a weekly minimum order, or a fine.
- **Insurance** (Bank & tax): premises cover pays back the till and repairs decor; machine cover makes repairs free.
  Claims make HMRC look a little harder.
- **A rival bakery**, Crumbs & Co, opens across the road once you're popular and takes a growing share of customers.
  Campaigns and low prices win them back; or buy them out.
- **Customers come in types**: regulars tip, commuters fill the station branch at rush hour, the café has a lunch rush,
  tourists come in summer (the gelateria most of all) and the maison is busiest at weekends. A food customer who also
  buys a drink pays a little extra for the combo.
- **The black-market auction**: once a week Dez auctions a few kits of a rare product (Blue Moon Crystal, Dragon's
  Breath, Golden Brick, Stardust) you can't get any other way.
- **Daily and weekly goals** (Orders): three a day and two a week, for cash and XP; weekly goals also pay a prestige token.
- **Weekly report card**: an A–F grade for the bakery, the back room and the books with every tax bill.
- **Prestige challenges**: when you start again, pick a harder run (no back room, doubled tax or doubled debt) for
  more tokens next time.

## Orders, milestones and critics

- **📦 Special orders** arrive every few hours: a wedding wants 60 croissants by tonight, a festival crew wants 20 bags.
  Deliver from any shop's case (or the back room) before the deadline for a big payment and XP. Miss one and popularity
  (or street rep) suffers. Grace delivers bakery orders for you; Lenny delivers Dez's jobs.
- **🏆 Milestones** pay a one-off reward: your first thousand customers, every recipe, five shops, a kingpin's worth of bags.
- **📝 Food critics** turn up without warning. A full case, charm and popularity win stars; the review moves customer
  numbers for two days.

## More to spend on

- **Machine tiers**: every machine, in the kitchens and the back room, upgrades five times. Kitchen tiers cook faster
  and make more servings; back-room tiers cook faster and purer.
- More crew: Chen the sous chef (+10% servings) and Imogen the marketing director (campaigns run 50% longer).

## No game over

Nothing ends the game. Trouble costs you instead:

- **Busted** (heat hits 100, or a raid finds product): fines, every bag and kit seized, police tape on the back room for 3 days, Jay charged, other night crew may quit, and a scandal in the papers. Popularity drops, and customers stay away for a few days.
- **Behind with the bank**: whatever you can't pay rolls over with 5% on top, then 0.5% a day interest. While you're behind, Grimshaw Recovery's heavies may visit. They smash decorations, crack tiles, tear wallpaper, wreck a machine or empty the till. Big Ron helps. Once you're two payments behind you can restructure the debt to call them off.
- **Cash never goes below zero.** If you're broke with nothing to bake, the market traders' association chips in so you can always start again.
- **Clearing the debt** is a milestone, not an ending. After that there's property to buy, from the stall's pitch to the whole market.

## Popularity and marketing

Popularity (⭐) decides how many customers come. It drifts back towards the middle over time. The 📣 Marketing tab helps
you win it back with a PR manager (Mel) and campaigns, from flyers to a documentary. Campaigns overlap, so each extra one adds a little less.

## XP and levels

Everything earns XP: each batch out of a machine (longer and higher-level recipes give more), each item sold, each
back-room cook and bag sold, courier orders and seeing off rivals. Each level pays a small bonus and unlocks something:
ingredients, machines, recipes, products, upgrades, crew, campaigns, property, bank features and design items.
Locked things show 🔒 and their level, and every card's ⓘ explains it.

## Designing the bakery

Open **🎨 Design** any time:

- **Floor**: tap single squares to lay tiles, or do the whole floor.
- **Walls**: wallpaper each wall panel.
- **Decor**: place plants, tables, a piano, an aquarium, a carousel and more on the green squares. Tap one to move, turn or sell it (half back).
- **Wall art**: paintings, mirrors, gold records and more on the wall panels.
- **Outside**: each shop's paint, wall finish (render, brick, timber cladding, stone, glazed tiles, marble), windows
  (plain, Georgian, arched, stained glass, neon or gold frames), front door (wood, green, red, glass, Victorian, revolving,
  gilded), roof (felt, gravel, pitched terracotta or slate, a green roof, solar panels, a rooftop terrace), the awning
  (its stripes also skirt the food carts and stalls), a shop sign (wooden, hand-painted, neon or gold) and extras
  (window boxes, bay trees, a bike rack, a bench, bunting, lanterns, string lights). All of it adds charm. Zoom out and the corner
  buttons become a single 🏠 Outside button, which opens these as tabs (Paint, Walls, Windows, Door, Roof, Awning, Sign,
  Extras); zoom back in and Map, Design and Menu return.
- **The building grows** when zoomed out: the front terrace gets a deeper awning and planters, the food carts café
  parasols, the side stalls a lean-to roof, the kitchen island roof lanterns (or roof windows on a pitched roof), the
  back-room benches extractor fans and ducting, the cellar a pavement grille, the annex a raised roof, and buying the
  flat upstairs adds a second storey to the stall.
- **Expand**: terraces for more squares, and the kitchen island, carts, stalls and back-room benches for more machines.
  You can see them: an island counter, striped food carts and stall canopies, steel benches, a cellar hatch and the annex.

Upgraded machines look it too: chrome bands, a glow strip, gold trim and a star at tier 5, with pips for the tier.

Decor adds **charm**. Each point of charm brings about 1% more customers, up to +30%.

## Balance simulator

Bots check in four times a day (8am, 12:30, 5:30pm, 9:30pm) with three strategies (honest, careful, greedy) and report
how far the bakery got: level by day, debt cleared, missed payments, busts, collector visits and cash.

```
node "C:\Users\khale\Documents\Claude projects\BakingBad\tools\sim.mjs" 3 365
```

`TRACE=careful` prints one run day by day. All numbers live in `www/js/data.js`.

## Layout

- `www/` is the whole game (plain HTML/CSS/JS, no build step). It is what gets packaged for Android.
- `www/js/scene.js` is the 3D scene (Three.js, kept in `www/vendor/` so the game works offline). All models are built from simple shapes in code.
- `www/js/design.js` holds the design catalogue and rules (squares, charm, expansions); `www/js/world.js` the neighbourhood.
- `www/js/data.js` holds the numbers and content, `www/js/game.js` the rules on the real clock (no DOM), and `www/js/ui.js` the screens and input.
- `tools/sim.mjs` is the balance simulator.
