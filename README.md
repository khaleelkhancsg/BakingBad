# Baking Bad

An idle bakery game on the real clock. You inherit Aunt Rosa's bakery stall at the night market, and £24,000 of her debt.
The bank wants £2,000 a week, starting after two weeks' grace. Dez, one of Rosa's regulars, knows what the back room used to be for.

The game never ends: like Clash of Clans, you just keep building the place up. Levels go up to 99.

It's a 3D game: you look down into the shop at an angle. The bakery has ovens, mixers, fryers and more, and a glass case
that fills with what you make while customers walk in and queue. Next door is the back room, lit purple. Tap a machine
(or the bubble above it) to work it. Drag to look around (a quick swipe glides on a little), and pinch or scroll to zoom: it zooms in on the point between your fingers (or under the mouse), and moving both fingers pans at the same time. A swipe that starts on a machine or its bubble just moves the view; only a tap opens it. Double-tap empty ground to glide back to the normal view. A tap on the scene closes the menu or map, and Esc (or the phone's back button) closes whatever is on top.

A compact strip along the top shows the time, cash, level, popularity, heat and anything that needs you. Zoom right out to see the whole building from outside (walls, windows, the sign and awning, the roofs, and a wide pavement all round that the terraces never swallow); zoom back in for the cut-away view. Tap the door between the shop and the back room (or its 🚪 bubble) to go through. Three buttons sit in the bottom-right corner: **☰ Menu** (in the shop: Kitchen, Supplies, Upgrades, Crew, Orders, Marketing, Shops & property,
Bank & tax; in the back room only back-room sections: Lab, Supplies (kits, cutting, the auction), Upgrades, Crew,
Deals (the back door, Dez's jobs, deliveries), Heat & police, Turf, Wash and Logistics), **🎨 Design** (in the back room: its benches, cellar and annex), and **🗺️ Map** to move between your shops and the back room.

## The real clock

There's no in-game day. The clock in the corner is your clock.

- **The bakery never closes.** Buy ingredients, cook, change prices, hire, decorate and expand whenever you like.
  Customers come all day and night, busiest at breakfast and lunch, quietest in the small hours.
- **The back room** can be used any time, but buyers only come to the back door in the evening, at night and in the
  early morning (6pm to 7am).
- **A new day starts at 6am**: rent and wages are paid, the bank takes its payment on payment day, the police decide
  whether to raid, the market sets new ingredient prices, and yesterday's report is filed (📋 in the HUD, or the Bank tab).
- **When you're away**, the game plays the time forward when you come back: customers buy from the case until it's
  empty, food left done in a machine spoils, interest builds on missed payments, crew do their jobs, and buyers come and
  go at the back door. "While you were away" sums it up. Up to 14 days are played; anything longer is skipped.

## Buying things

- **Ingredients**: use − and + to fill the basket (Stock up adds three batches of everything on the counters), then Checkout.
- **Design**: tiles, wallpaper, decor and wall art show in the shop as you place them but are only paid for when you
  press Checkout; Cancel puts everything back.
- **Big purchases** (machines, tiers, expansions, shops, vans, fronts, corners, property, upgrades and recipes) ask
  you to confirm first. Buying a machine from 🍳 Kitchen or 🧪 Lab takes you to place it, then back to that section.
- **Machines**: a machine's menu has a header that stays at the top while you scroll: ‹ back to the section you opened it
  from, and ✕. An ⓘ opened from a machine's menu closes back to that menu ("‹ Back to the oven"), where you left it. The Lab's cards show, like the Kitchen's, how many of each machine you have, how many of its products
  you've unlocked, and what each one is doing.

## Bills

Wages come out every morning; everything else is a monthly bill, paid at 6am every 30 days (the first a month after you
take over), itemised in 🏦 Bank & tax with the date it's due:
- **Rent**: each shop's (the stall's is £1,200 a month), plus business rates on the branches.
- **Council tax** on the flat upstairs: £140.
- **Utilities**: power, gas and water, £90 a shop plus £18 for every kitchen machine and £45 for every back-room one
  (grow lights and burners run hot).

Whatever you can't cover goes on what you owe the bank. **Buying back Rosa's stall** (🏪 Shops & property, £40,000)
ends its rent, but the bank holds the deeds until the debt is cleared.

## The kitchen

Every machine has its own menu. Tap one to cook, set prices and learn recipes. Each batch is one
timer on one machine. When it's done, tap ✓ to put it on the counters. Leave it too long and it spoils: food keeps for about
as long as it took to cook (at least 30 minutes).

Anything you make, in the bakery or the back room, takes from a minute to two days, and the longer it takes the more
a batch earns: more servings (or bags) and a higher price for slow-made goods. A 2-minute batch of cookies makes about
£18, a 2-hour sourdough £113, an 8-hour flatbread £449, and the two-day showpieces at the top thousands. The longest
cooks unlock late: times stretch with a recipe's level (up to four times by level 99), while each recipe keeps its place
among its neighbours, so there are quick bakes at every level. Per hour, short cooks still pay best while you're
playing; long ones keep the counters and the back door stocked while you're out or asleep. In the back room what a cook
is worth comes from its level and time alone (no product is a shortcut), its bag count grows like a batch's servings,
and the price per bag makes up the rest. Special orders always give you time to cook what they ask for. Heat follows the money too: every cook adds heat from its price per bag, plus a little more for big cooks (over £20,000), up to 8 a cook, and every bag sold adds 0.05.

The stall only takes the first four; the rest are fitted in Rosa's branches (see Shops). You can run at most three of any machine in a shop.

205 recipes in all, from scones and lattes to pho, Beef Wellington and a golden croquembouche. You can only own as many of each machine as your level allows, and only as many as you have room for:
expansions (Design → Expand) add room for more, so a room never fills up before it has grown. The bakery starts with room
for 6 machines; the kitchen refit (level 7) adds 4, the island 2 and the bigger island 2 more, then food carts and side
stalls 5 each (on the terraces or anywhere). The basement starts with room for 4; clearing the coal store (level 14) adds
one more, then the benches, the cold store, the far wall, and digging out under the front and side terraces 3 each. At the
limit the buy button says "Reach level N to unlock additional ovens".

**Placing machines and counters.** Nothing has a fixed spot: you choose where every machine and counter stands. Buying a
machine (☰ Menu → Kitchen, "+1 · place it") opens 🎨 Design → 🍳 Kitchen with it picked up: the floor turns green where it
can go and red where it can't, and you pay when you tap a green square. Counters are bought the same way (the 🧁 Counter
in the Kitchen tab). Tap any machine, counter or the till in Design to **Move** it (it carries on cooking) or **Turn** it. Each faces
away from the wall it's against to start with. The rules keep the room workable: nothing goes on the shop door
or the door to the back room, or on top of anything else; the square in front of every machine must stay reachable (that's where you work it), and
so must the front of every counter and the till (where customers stand). Machines out on a terrace stand on a low wooden
deck. In the basement, 🎨 Design → 🧪 Machines does the same, with everything reachable from the foot of the stairs and
the stairs' landing, the buyers' spots and the tables kept clear. You, Priya, customers and buyers all walk around
whatever you've put down; customers queue in front of the counter they want, wherever it is.

**The street.** Customers walk into town from its faded edges along the pavements (along the front, down the side
street, or over the zebra crossing from the far side), go in, queue at the counter they want, come out and walk off
into the distance again. Window-shoppers do the same without buying, and a few passers-by just walk through. The shop's front door (seen when you zoom out; close up the doorway is open) swings open as people come and go, and passing cars brake for anyone on the zebra crossing. The cars are ready-made low-poly models (a GT-R, a Charger, a Rolls-Royce, a Camaro, a hatchback, an SUV and a sports car; see Credits) in random colours, with spinning wheels, brake lights and lamps that glow at night; they appear a moment after the town does. The town around the shop is 84 m across: a taller street behind, blocks to either side and low shops across the main road. Up to nine
customers are shown walking at once; the sales themselves happen in the engine whether or not anyone is drawn.
Everything solid in the scene carries a simple generated prototype texture (`three-helpers.js`): wood grain, floorboards,
bark, brushed steel, chequer plate, card, fabric, velvet, fur, leaves, stone, concrete, plaster, tiles, rubber, epoxy,
breeze block, brick, acoustic foam and gold leaf. Models name theirs with `tmat(colour, kind)`; anything else gets one
picked by its material's colour (food, people, glass and lights stay plain). Every decoration and wall-art piece has
its own model. The basement's floor and wall designs each have their own pattern, laid out by the metre (`worldUV`) so
it doesn't stretch along long walls.

**Display counters.** There's no menu to manage: anything you've learned (and is in season) can be cooked, and when
it's collected it goes onto the glass counters by the till by itself. Each counter holds one product, 4 batches of it.
A batch first tops up a counter already showing that product; if that would go over, it fills it to the top and the
rest goes onto a free counter; with no free counter left, what didn't fit waits in the machine (✓ still showing) until
there's room, and spoils on its usual timer. A counter that sells out is free again, and so is a product's extra counter
once what's left fits on fewer. Every branch starts with 2 counters and can buy up to 7 (levels 2–22, £250 to £12,000);
buy one in 🎨 Design → 🍳 Kitchen and tap where it goes. Tap a stocked counter to see how many servings are on it (and
on all its counters), its price and how many it holds. Every recipe has its own little model on the counter, and the
more different things are out the more customers come (variety, up to +40% with 7). Counters running low show an amber
⚠️. The counter window has **Restock** (starts a free machine on it), **today's special** (one per shop: 20% dearer and
twice as many people ask for it, with a gold star over the counter), **Throw away** (all of it, freeing its counters)
and, from level 10, a **bigger cabinet** for that counter (+2 batches, £1,500).

The same level caps go for how many kits Dez sells you a day, how many decorations you can place, how many campaigns you can run and how much the bank lends.

When lots of machines crowd the screen, idle ones show as small dots. Crew make idle play work: Priya the baker puts finished food in the case, Marco the head chef starts the same recipe
again whenever its tray has room, and Grace the manager keeps the ingredients topped up.

## The back room

The back room is a small room off the shop with the back door and a stairwell. The stairs go down to the **basement**,
which runs under the whole shop: that's where the machines, the bag and cash tables, the kit boxes and the decor are,
and where buyers come (in at the back door and down the stairs). Tap the 🪜 Basement bubble on the door, or the stairs,
to go down: the view dips briefly to dark and comes back on the basement right underneath, with the shop and the street
in front of it cut away (the buildings behind stay in view). One flight of stairs runs from the landing by the back door,
under the shop's side wall, and comes into the basement through an opening in its left wall; buyers walk down it. ☀️ Upstairs brings it all back. Digging out under the front and side terraces (Design → Layout, once you own
those terraces) makes it 2 m bigger each way.

Say yes to Dez and the stall's back room is yours. Every branch you open has a back room of its own with its own
machines, but only the stall has buyers: branch back rooms pack what they make into Rosa's vans, which drive it to
the stall under the bakery's own deliveries (see Logistics). Bags, kits, heat, turf and laundering are shared. Buy kits from Dez, cook them on the back-room machines, and sell the bags to
buyers at the back door after dark. The products are real drugs with serious names. How they're made is abstracted to
a machine and a timer, but each supply kit lists what really goes in for growing, sourcing, cutting, pressing or
mixing (methamphetamine's chemistry stays a supplier's "precursor kit"), and each product says what it's usually cut with.

- **Purity** decides what a bag is worth (60% sells at the list price). It comes from the machine's tier, back-room
  upgrades (scales, extractor hood, filters, purity tester), Doc and Pip.
- **Cutting** (Pure, Light, Heavy) gives more bags from a cook, each worth less.
- **Street rep** decides how many buyers come. Pure product raises it; cut or weak product and busts lower it.
  Buyers mostly come for what you're known to have in stock; if you're out, Jay or Lena offers them something else
  and about half take it.
- **Heat** cools by itself a little faster every level (0.27 an hour at level 1, about 1.25 at level 50), so a bigger
  operation can run without the police closing in, as long as you don't push it.
- **Turf** (🗺️): buy corners around town, from the estate to penthouse parties. Give each a product and stock it; they
  sell while you're away, faster at night. Police sweeps and rival crews can take what's on a corner.
- **Dez** buys anything you can't shift, at half price.
- **Night crew**: Jay sells to buyers you miss (keeps 20%), Lena the host does it better (keeps 10%, never serves anyone
  flagged), Ash spots officers, Sam bags finished cooks, Doc starts the next one, Kai keeps the corners stocked, DS Hale
  tips you off about raids, Big Ron sees off rivals.


## Logistics

- **Vans** (Menu → 🚚 Logistics): the first comes with your first branch. Buy more as you level up, make them bigger,
  and fit hidden compartments. A run takes 1 hour from the café up to 4 from the maison.
- **Stop and search**: any run can be stopped (more likely when heat is high or the load is big). If police find the
  load it's seized, the van impounded for two days and the driver arrested. Marnie the dispatcher sends vans for you.
- **Delivery rounds** (level 45): buyers phone instead of knocking and riders take the product to them. Most undercover
  officers never get past the call, there's less heat around the stall, raids are less likely and buyers pay 10% more,
  but riders can be stopped and searched or walk into a sting: the bags are lost and the rider arrested.

## Clean and dirty money

- **Clean cash** pays for everything above board: ingredients, machines, recipes, decor, crew, campaigns, shops and the bank.
- **Dirty cash** comes easier (the back room pays well) but buys less: the back room's machines, tiers and upgrades, the
  night crew, bribes and Dez's kits. Dez charges half as much again if you pay him in clean money.
- **Holding dirty cash is a risk**: raids, rival crews and busts all take a share of it.
- **Laundering is slow and costs a cut.** Each day only about a third of the day's takings across your shops (plus what
  your branches can front) passes as normal; more than that adds heat. The wash takes 25%; Viv the accountant, creative
  accounting and a shell company take less.

## Washing money

Menu → 🧼 Wash lists every route. Each has its own daily limit, cut, speed, tax treatment and risk, so spreading
money across them keeps heat and HMRC's questions down:

| 🏧 Smurfing | 8 | 10% | no | slow (trickles in all day, up to a daily limit); banks sometimes flag deposits |
| 🎰 The casino | 25 | 15% | no (gambling winnings aren't taxed) | casinos report big spenders |
| 🏝️ Offshore account | 35 | 5% in, 15% out clean | yes, as consultancy fees | dirty cash parked there is safe from raids, busts and rivals |

Fronts run from a launderette (£1,500 a night) to a nightclub (£200,000). A pile of unwashed dirty cash (over £5,000) counts as extra heat when police decide whether to raid, so washing or stashing it lowers the odds of a bust. HMRC notice front money half as much as
till money; an investigation or a bust can close a front for a few days. Felix the money man spreads your dirty cash
across the casino, cheap crypto and the art dealer every morning. A neat paper trail makes investigations less likely;
a friendly bank manager doubles smurfing limits.

## Tax

Inspired by the UK system, but settled weekly (a real tax year would take a real year). HMRC's bill comes at 6am once
a week and is paid from clean cash; see it building up in the 🏦 Bank tab.

- **VAT (20%)** once your declared takings pass £90,000. Hot food and drinks are standard-rated; cold takeaway food and
  bread are zero-rated, as in the UK.
- **Corporation tax**: 19% on small profits, 25% above the main limit, with marginal relief in between. Ingredients,
  rent, wages, machines and everything else you spend on the business count against profit.
- **Employer National Insurance (15%)** on day-crew wages above the threshold.
- **Business rates** on every branch; the stall gets small business rates relief.
- **Laundered money is declared income**, so it's taxed too.
- **Declaring less** (90%, 75% or half) cuts the bill but raises the chance of an investigation. If HMRC find undeclared
  income you pay it back twice over and it makes the papers. Viv the accountant trims the bill and halves the chance.
- **Unpaid tax** gets a 5% penalty and daily interest, and HMRC take what they can each morning.

## Designing the back rooms

In a back room, 🎨 Design has its own tabs (paid in dirty cash, through the same basket and checkout):

- **Floor** and **Walls**: whole-room styles, from bare concrete and breeze block to lab epoxy, soundproof foam and gold leaf.
- **Machines**: buy back-room machines onto a square, and move or turn the ones you have.
- **Decor** on the back room's free squares (the stairs, where buyers stand and the tables stay clear). Pieces do
  something, added up across all your back rooms:
  - 😌 **Comfort** (sofas, a big telly, a pool table, a fish tank, a jukebox): the night crew gain loyalty faster.
  - 🫥 **Cover** (bread crates, flour shelving, a wall safe, a false wall): raids find product less often (up to 40% less).
  - 🛡️ **Security** (CCTV monitors, a reinforced door, a panic room): heat cools faster (up to 50%).
- **Layout**: clear the coal store first, then the benches, cold store and the far wall for room for more machines, and
  dig out under the front and side terraces to make the basement bigger. Decor can't go where it would cut anyone off from a machine, a door or the buyers (those squares show
  faint red); decor already in the way when the room changes comes down with a full refund.
- **Walking**: people walk around machines, counters and decor. If there's no way through they stop as close as they
  can get rather than walking through things.

## Baking well

- **Perfect bakes**: collect a batch yourself within a few minutes of it finishing (15% of its cook time, between 2 and
  10 minutes) and it's perfect: 20% more servings and half as much XP again. Crew collecting don't get them, except
  a sharp baker half the time.
- **Mastery**: every batch of a recipe counts. Stars at 15, 50, 120, 250 and 500 batches each add 3% servings and 2%
  to the price; five stars make it a signature dish, with a gold sparkle over its counter.
- **Throwing food away**: tap a counter and throw its product away; its counters go free for whatever you cook next.

## Seasons

Seasons follow the real calendar: Valentine's (1–14 Feb), Easter (20 Mar–20 Apr), summer (15 Jun–31 Aug), Halloween
(15–31 Oct), Bonfire Night (1–7 Nov) and Christmas (1–26 Dec). Each brings three to five limited recipes on the
stall's machines (learned free when it starts, cookable only until it ends, and a bit dearer), shifts what people want
(ice cream in summer, pastries at Christmas), and decorates the shop: a garland along the back wall and something by
the door (a tree, pumpkins, a heart balloon, an Easter basket, a parasol, lanterns).

## The story and the collection book

Chapters turn up as you go: Rosa's letters and keepsakes, Dez's past catching up with him, and DI Harrow's case as it
builds (14 in all). Most end with a choice of two: pin Rosa's letter by the till or pocket what's folded behind it,
call in Dez's old debt or let it go, make Harrow a coffee or keep the back door shut while he's there. Each one nudges
things a little and never decides anything big: a few points of popularity or heat (which drift back anyway), a little
evidence, a kit or two, or some cash scaled to your level. The chapter says what it did, and re-reading it in the
Collection shows what you chose. Telling Dez you're not interested the first time puts the bakery first (popularity
+2); he comes back a few days later, leaving it a little longer each time you say no.
☰ Menu → 📖 Collection keeps the chapters to read again, and has sets to complete for prize money:
recipes baked, signature dishes, perfect bakes, seasonal treats, products made, chapters and bakery awards.

## Locals, managers and the rival

- **Local tastes**: each branch's customers like different things (market shoppers: bread and pastries; students:
  drinks and ice cream, and they watch their money; office workers: savoury; commuters: drinks; tourists; day-trippers:
  cold treats; the rich don't mind prices). Shops & property and the Kitchen say who's local.
- **Branch managers** (from level 10): hire one for any branch (junior, experienced or star: £150, £400 or £1,000 a day).
  They collect and restock that shop's counters every 30, 15 or 5 minutes while you're elsewhere.
- **Crumbs & Co fights back**: while they're open, now and then they start a price war on your best seller (fewer
  people pay full price for 2 days, unless it's today's special or priced low), try to poach one of your day crew (match
  the offer or lose them), or tip off a critic to visit today. If you have a back room, Jay may offer to have the crew
  "have a word" with their suppliers: they lose customers fast, but heat and evidence go up.

## The night: patrols and the crew

- **Patrol forecast** (Heat & police): at 6am you get tonight's patrols for the evening, late night and early hours:
  quiet, normal or heavy. Heavy patrols raise the chance a buyer is undercover, corner sweeps and arrests; quiet ones
  lower them.
- **Crew personalities**: each crew member is steady, loyal, greedy, nervous, chatty or sharp, shown on their card.
  Loyal never talk; greedy want more pay; nervous get picked up more; chatty bring day customers in but turn informant
  more easily; sharp collect perfectly or sell for more.
- **Informants**: night crew with low loyalty may start talking to Harrow, feeding his case every day until their
  loyalty is won back (a bonus) or you let them go. You only learn who it is with a lawyer or a bent copper.

## Rush hour, seasonal customers and the late game

- **Rush hour**: once a day, at a random time between 11am and 6pm, twice as many customers come for an hour. The top bar
  warns 30 minutes before (🔔 and the time). Keep the counters stocked: serve plenty with hardly anyone turned away and
  you get a prize (grows with level) and popularity.
- **Seasonal customers**: during a season, special orders often ask for the season's recipes (the bonfire on the common,
  the office Christmas party...) and pay 40% more.
- **Landmarks** (Shops & property, from level 50): Rosa's baking school (+1 batch on every counter), a flagship
  renovation (+15% customers), Rosa's in the supermarkets (+£6,000 a day), a baking show (+20% customers, prices 5% up),
  the Rosa Foundation (popularity never below 60, heat from cooking rises 15% slower) and Rosa Tower (+£60,000 a day,
  +10% customers).
- **Investment fund** (from level 30): put clean cash in and it grows 0.3% a day; take it out whenever you like.
- **Branch back doors**: in a branch's basement layout, "Open a back door here" (level 25) brings buyers to that branch
  after dark (fewer than at the stall), and what its basement makes goes straight into your bags instead of the vans.

## A clean record

Days in a row with nothing cooked, sold or washed after dark count as a clean record (Bank & tax shows it). Every 3
clean days bring 1% more customers, up to +20%; after 14 days critics look on you kindly; and bakery awards come at 7,
30, 90 and 180 days (Local favourite, Best bakery in town, Regional baker of the year, National treasure), with prize
money that grows with your level. Any night trade starts the count again.

## Settings, the guide and sound

☰ Menu → ⚙️ Settings: sound on or off, phone notifications on or off, save backups, the first-day guide, and starting
over. Settings stay the same through new games and prestige.
- **Save backup**: Back up shows your whole game as a code (copy it, or save it as a file in the browser); Load a backup
  pastes one back in, replacing the game you have.
- **First-day guide**: new games get eight short steps in a card above the corner buttons (bake, collect, the counters,
  supplies, buying a counter, design, the bank, and a hint about the back). Each moves on by itself once done.
- **Sound**: all made in the browser: the till, the oven timer, the shop bell for orders, a knock at the back door, coins,
  level-ups, a siren when heat gets high, and a low hum in the back room at night.
- **Notifications** (phone app): when the app goes to the background it sets reminders for when the kitchen is done,
  food is 10 minutes from spoiling, the back room's cooks are done, the back door opens, and a bank payment is due; they
  clear when you come back.

## Prestige

From level 25, Menu → Bank & tax → Prestige lets you hand the business on and start again from nothing: no shops,
machines, cash or crew, and a fresh debt at Aunt Rosa's stall. In return you get **prestige tokens** for what the
business was worth (the square root of net worth over £10,000: £1M is worth 10, £100M is worth 100). Every token ever
earned adds 1% to every sale and bag, for good. Spend tokens on perks that make each fresh start quicker:

| Perk | Each level |
|---|---|
| 💰 Rosa's savings | start with £2,500 more |
| 🏦 Good credit | inherit £2,500 less debt |
| 🎓 Quick learner | +10% XP |
| 🫶 Loyal regulars | +5% customers |
| 📜 Family recipes | start knowing every recipe up to 5 levels higher |
| 🏷️ Trade discount | machines 5% cheaper |
| 🚀 A head start | start two levels higher |
| 🤝 Old contacts | back room open from day one, 2 more kits a day, kits 5% cheaper |
| 🕶️ Street legend | +10 street rep, +5% on every bag |

Perks cost more tokens with each level. Perks and tokens carry over every reset; everything else (milestones
included) starts again.

## The long game

- **Crew loyalty**: every hire has loyalty and greed (greedy ones cost more). Loyalty grows while they're paid; a bonus
  (a day's pay) buys more. Night crew below 30 may talk to the detective.
- **Decisions** pop up and wait for you (❗ in the top bar): bail for an arrested dealer or driver (pay to keep the crew
  loyal, or cut them loose for less heat), and now and then a bulk deal, a journalist, a health inspector, a package
  Dez wants you to hide, or a charity appeal. Ignore one and the safe option is taken.
- **DI Harrow's case** (🕵️ in the top bar): stings, seized vans and deliveries, corner sweeps, raids, informants and HMRC
  finds all add evidence; lying low lets it fade. At 100% it goes to court: big fines, everything seized, back rooms
  sealed for a week and most of the night crew sent down. Bribe the detective or frame a rival crew to knock it back.
- **Heat by area**: vans and the streets (corners and riders) have their own heat, which cools faster than the back room's.
- **Supplier contracts** (Supplies): ingredients 15–20% cheaper for a weekly minimum order, or a fine.
- **Insurance** (Bank & tax): premises cover pays back the till and repairs decor; machine cover makes repairs free.
  Claims make HMRC look a little harder.
- **A rival bakery**, Crumbs & Co, opens across the road once you're popular and takes a growing share of customers.
  Campaigns and low prices win them back; or buy them out.
- **Customers come in types**: regulars tip, commuters fill the station branch at rush hour, the café has a lunch rush,
  tourists come in summer (the gelateria most of all) and the maison is busiest at weekends. A food customer who also
  buys a drink pays a little extra for the combo.
- **The black-market auction**: once a week Dez auctions a few kits of a rare product (blue crystal methamphetamine,
  a rare Gelato strain, a stamped Colombian kilo, China White heroin) you can't get any other way.
- **Daily and weekly goals** (Orders): three a day and two a week, for cash and XP; weekly goals also pay a prestige token.
- **Weekly report card**: an A–F grade for the bakery, the back room and the books with every tax bill.
- **Prestige challenges**: when you start again, pick a harder run (no back room, doubled tax or doubled debt) for
  more tokens next time.

## Orders, milestones and critics

- **📦 Special orders** arrive every few hours: a wedding wants 60 croissants by tonight, a festival crew wants 20 bags.
  Deliver from any shop's case (or the back room) before the deadline for a big payment and XP. Miss one and popularity
  (or street rep) suffers. Grace delivers bakery orders for you; Lenny delivers Dez's jobs.
- **🏆 Milestones** pay a one-off reward: your first thousand customers, every recipe, five shops, a kingpin's worth of bags.
- **📝 Food critics** turn up without warning. A full case, charm and popularity win stars; the review moves customer
  numbers for two days.

## More to spend on

- **Machine tiers**: every machine, in the kitchens and the back room, upgrades five times. Kitchen tiers cook faster
  and make more servings; back-room tiers cook faster and purer.
- More crew: Chen the sous chef (+10% servings) and Imogen the marketing director (campaigns run 50% longer).

## No game over

Nothing ends the game. Trouble costs you instead:

- **Busted** (heat hits 100, or a raid finds product): fines, every bag and kit seized, police tape on the back room for 3 days, Jay charged, other night crew may quit, and a scandal in the papers. Popularity drops, and customers stay away for a few days.
- **Behind with the bank**: whatever you can't pay rolls over with 5% on top, then 0.5% a day interest. While you're behind, Grimshaw Recovery's heavies may visit. They smash decorations, crack tiles, tear wallpaper, wreck a machine or empty the till. Big Ron helps. Once you're two payments behind you can restructure the debt to call them off.
- **Cash never goes below zero.** If you're broke with nothing to bake, the market traders' association chips in so you can always start again.
- **Clearing the debt** is a milestone, not an ending. After that there's property to buy, from Rosa's stall itself to the whole market.

## Popularity and marketing

Popularity (⭐) decides how many customers come. It drifts back towards the middle over time. The 📣 Marketing tab helps
you win it back with a PR manager (Mel) and campaigns, from flyers to a documentary. Campaigns overlap, so each extra one adds a little less.

## XP and levels

Everything earns XP: each batch out of a machine (longer and higher-level recipes give more), each item sold, each
back-room cook and bag sold, courier orders and seeing off rivals. Each level pays a small bonus and unlocks something:
ingredients, machines, recipes, products, upgrades, crew, campaigns, property, bank features and design items.
Locked things show 🔒 and their level, and every card's ⓘ explains it.

## Designing the bakery

Open **🎨 Design** any time:

- **Floor**: tap single squares to lay tiles, or do the whole floor.
- **Walls**: wallpaper each wall panel.
- **Kitchen**: buy machines and counters onto a square, and move or turn the ones you have (see Placing machines and counters).
- **Decor**: place plants, tables, a piano, an aquarium, a carousel and more on the green squares (anywhere that keeps the machines, counters, till and doors reachable; rugs can go anywhere free). Tap one to move, turn or sell it (half back).
- **Wall art**: paintings, mirrors, gold records and more on the wall panels.
- **Outside**: each shop's paint, wall finish (render, brick, timber cladding, stone, glazed tiles, marble), windows
  (plain, Georgian, arched, stained glass, neon or gold frames), front door (wood, green, red, glass, Victorian, revolving,
  gilded), roof (felt, gravel, pitched terracotta or slate, a green roof, solar panels, a rooftop terrace), the awning
  (its stripes also skirt the food carts and stalls), a shop sign (wooden, hand-painted, neon or gold) and extras
  (window boxes, bay trees, a bike rack, a bench, bunting, lanterns, string lights). All of it adds charm. Zoom out and the corner
  buttons become a single 🏠 Outside button, which opens these as tabs (Paint, Walls, Windows, Door, Roof, Awning, Sign,
  Extras); zoom back in and Map, Design and Menu return.
- **The building grows** when zoomed out: the front terrace gets a deeper awning (over the food carts) and
  planters, the side stalls a lean-to roof, the kitchen island roof lanterns (or roof windows on a pitched roof), the
  back-room benches extractor fans and ducting, the cellar a pavement grille, the annex a raised roof, and buying the
  flat upstairs adds a second storey to the stall (its windows light up in the evening and early morning). The OPEN sign
  on the door turns to CLOSED when trade stops for the night, and with a revolving door people keep to one side going in
  and the other coming out while the wings turn with them.
- **Expand**: terraces for more squares, and the kitchen island, carts, stalls and back-room benches for room for more
  machines. From outside you can see them: parasols, the stalls' lean-to roof, roof lanterns, extractor fans, a cellar grille.

Upgraded machines look it too: chrome bands, a glow strip, gold trim and a star at tier 5, with pips for the tier.

Decor adds **charm**. Each point of charm brings about 1% more customers, up to +30%.

## Balance simulator

Bots check in four times a day (8am, 12:30, 5:30pm, 9:30pm) with three strategies (honest, careful, greedy) and report
how far the bakery got: level by day, debt cleared, missed payments, busts, collector visits and cash.

```
node "C:\Users\khale\Documents\Claude projects\BakingBad\tools\sim.mjs" 3 365
```

`TRACE=careful` prints one run day by day. All numbers live in `www/js/data.js`.

`node tools/fuzz.mjs [runs] [days]` plays games with random actions (cooking, counters, specials, cabinets,
managers, expansions, decor, branches, decisions, saving and loading) and reports any broken rule: negative or NaN
money, a broken layout (two things on a square, a machine, counter, till or door nobody can reach), more machines than a room allows, stock with no counter, and so on.

`node tools/crewcheck.mjs` runs the game with and without each crew member and each upgrade and checks it does what
its card says: Priya collects every machine type in every shop onto the counters (and the rest waits when they're
full), Marco restarts, Sam and Doc bag and restart cooks in every shop, Grace buys what the machines cook, Jay and Lena
serve the back door, every upgrade moves the number it names, and so on (78 checks).

## Layout

- `www/` is the whole game (plain HTML/CSS/JS, no build step). It is what gets packaged for Android.
- `www/js/scene.js` is the 3D scene (Three.js, kept in `www/vendor/` so the game works offline). Almost everything is built from simple shapes in code; the cars are ready-made models (`www/models/cars/`, loaded by `www/js/carModels.js`, see Credits). The shop is lit bright, warm and steady at every hour; the back room keeps its own bulb (with the odd flicker) and purple neon. Each room's lights are only on while that room is showing, since light passes through the floor between them.
- `www/js/design.js` holds the design catalogue and rules (squares, charm, expansions); `www/js/world.js` the neighbourhood.
- `www/style.css` is the look, in one layer (one rule per selector), kept low-key to sit with the scene's lighting: warm espresso panels with cream text, serif headings and a thin copper-and-cream awning stripe along the top; in the back room the panels turn plum with a violet stripe. Each card's first line under its name is the thing that matters (the effect, the pay, the price), and recipes show their time, servings at price and profit a batch as labels (back-room products: time, bags at price, purity and profit a cook). The menu label and the ⓘ always use the same sum: takings less today's ingredients, or less the kit at Dez's dirty price, on that machine's tier. Sections open as a two-column grid that scrolls up and down. The labels over machines, counters, the stairs and people are small espresso tags with a tail pointing at what they're about: a copper edge for something to start, mint when it's ready, butter when it's low, red when it's burning or empty, plum in the back room. Counts on the menu and corner buttons are copper pills.
- `www/js/data.js` holds the numbers and content, `www/js/game.js` the rules on the real clock (no DOM), and `www/js/ui.js` the screens and input.
- `tools/sim.mjs` is the balance simulator.

## Android

`www/` is wrapped with Capacitor 8 (`capacitor.config.json`, app id `com.khaleel.bakingbad`) into the Android project
in `android/`. In the app:
- it runs full screen in portrait (swipe in from the edge for the system bars);
- the back button closes whatever is open one layer at a time (a question, a window, Design with its unpaid basket
  undone, a section, the menu or map), then goes from the back room to the shop, and only then sends the app to the
  background; it keeps its place and catches up on the real clock when you come back;
- it saves when it goes to the background. Saves live in the app's own storage, kept until you uninstall it or clear
  its data.

Building needs JDK 21 and the Android SDK (platform 36, build tools 36, platform-tools). On this PC the SDK is in
`C:\Users\khale\AppData\Local\Android\Sdk`, and `android/local.properties` (kept out of git) points the build at it.
On another machine, install Android Studio or the command-line tools and set `sdk.dir` there. Then:

```
npm run android:build
```

That copies `www/` into the app and builds `android/app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk`, which can be
sideloaded: copy it to the phone and open it (allow installs from that app when asked), or with USB debugging on and
the phone plugged in, `adb install -r android\app\build\outputs\apk\debug\app-debug.apk` (adb is in the SDK's
`platform-tools` folder). Installing again over the top keeps the save. `npm run android:sync` only copies `www/` across
(run it after changing the game); `npm run android:open` opens the project in Android Studio.
